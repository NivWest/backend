## What is the Sharpe Ratio?

The Sharpe ratio is a foundational financial metric that evaluates an investment portfolio's return relative to its risk. Named after Nobel laureate William F. Sharpe, it quantifies how much excess return an investor receives for each additional unit of portfolio volatility. In TradeEdu, the analytics engine tracks your Sharpe ratio alongside raw performance, emphasizing that generating returns through reckless leverage and erratic bets is inferior to disciplined, consistent wealth accumulation.

## How the Sharpe Ratio is Calculated

The Sharpe ratio is computed by subtracting the annualized risk-free rate from the annualized portfolio return, and dividing the difference by the annualized standard deviation of daily portfolio returns. For example, if your portfolio generated an 18% return against a 2% Swedish risk-free benchmark with an annualized volatility of 16%, your Sharpe ratio is exactly 1.00. A Sharpe ratio below 1.0 suggests insufficient compensation for volatility, while ratios above 1.5 indicate superior risk efficiency.

## Practical Interpretation in Trader Evaluation

Evaluating traders purely on gross profits ignores whether success resulted from skill or lucky gambles that nearly blew up the account. A trader who gained 15% with smooth, steady monthly compounding will possess a much higher Sharpe ratio than one who gained 20% after enduring wild 50% drawdowns. Focus on optimizing your Sharpe ratio to cultivate sustainable long-term trading habits.
