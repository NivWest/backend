## What is Volatility?

Volatility is a statistical measure of the dispersion of returns for a given financial security or market index. It quantifies how rapidly and dramatically an asset's price swings around its historical average return. Higher volatility indicates that a security's value can potentially spread out over a broad range of prices in a short timeframe, representing elevated uncertainty and investment risk.

## Historical versus Implied Volatility

Financial analysts distinguish between historical volatility and implied volatility. Historical volatility calculates the actual past price fluctuations by computing the annualized standard deviation of daily log returns over a specific lookback window, such as 30 or 252 trading days. Implied volatility, in contrast, is derived from options pricing models and reflects the collective forward-looking expectations of market participants regarding future turbulence.

## Managing Volatility in Portfolio Design

While novice traders often perceive volatility purely as danger, disciplined investors view volatility as a natural characteristic of market pricing that presents buying and selling opportunities. Controlling exposure through smaller position sizing, asset class diversification, and automated stop-loss thresholds protects the portfolio against volatile downswings while allowing patient capital to compound effectively.
