## What is Dividend Yield?

Dividend yield is a financial ratio that shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its current share price. For income-focused investors, dividends represent direct tangible returns paid out of net corporate earnings, allowing shareholders to compound wealth through cash payouts without having to liquidate underlying share holdings.

## Calculating Dividend Yield

The dividend yield formula divides the total annual cash dividends per share by the current market price per share, expressed as a percentage. For example, if a Nordic corporation pays an annual dividend of 4.00 SEK and its stock currently trades at 100.00 SEK, the dividend yield is exactly 4.00%. Because share prices fluctuate constantly on the exchange, dividend yields move inversely with price: as a stock falls, its yield rises, assuming dividend payments remain stable.

## Evaluating Dividend Sustainability and Traps

A high dividend yield is not universally positive. A dangerously inflated yield often flags a "dividend trap", where a steep collapse in share price elevates the mathematical yield right before the board of directors cuts or eliminates the payout. Prudent investors always inspect the dividend payout ratio—the proportion of net income allocated to dividends—to ensure operating earnings comfortably cover ongoing distributions.
