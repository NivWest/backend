## What is the P/E Ratio?

The price-to-earnings ratio, commonly abbreviated as the P/E ratio, is one of the most widely utilized valuation multiples in equity analysis. It measures a company's current share price relative to its per-share earnings. In simple terms, the P/E ratio indicates how many kronor investors are currently willing to pay for every single krona of corporate profit generated by the firm over the preceding twelve months.

## Calculating the P/E Ratio

The formula for the P/E ratio divides the market price per share by the company's earnings per share (EPS). For instance, if an equity like Fjord Robotics trades at 100 SEK per share and generated 5.00 SEK in earnings per share over the past year, its P/E ratio is 20.00. A high P/E ratio usually implies that investors anticipate substantial future earnings expansion, whereas a low P/E ratio may suggest either that the company is undervalued or facing systemic business headwinds.

## Limitations and Sector Context

P/E ratios must always be interpreted in the context of industry norms and cyclicality. Fast-growing software companies regularly command P/E ratios exceeding 40 because their profit margins expand rapidly at scale, whereas capital-intensive industrial and utility businesses typically trade at modest multiples around 12 to 15. Never compare P/E ratios across disparate sectors without adjusting for capital structure and growth trajectory.
