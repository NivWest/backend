## What is Portfolio Rebalancing?

Portfolio rebalancing is the systematic process of buying or selling assets in an investment portfolio to restore the original or desired asset allocation mix. Over time, high-performing positions naturally expand in value while underperforming assets contract, altering the proportional weighting of holdings. Without periodic rebalancing, a conservative portfolio can inadvertently drift into an aggressive, over-concentrated posture.

## How Rebalancing Enforces "Buy Low, Sell High"

The fundamental psychological virtue of rebalancing is that it compels investors to sell portions of appreciated winners and redirect capital into lagging sectors or cash reserves. Human emotion naturally urges traders to chase outperforming stocks at market tops and abandon underperforming assets at cyclical bottoms. Rebalancing creates a mechanical, emotion-free framework that systematically locks in capital gains and purchases undervalued assets.

## Frequency and Threshold Strategies

Investors typically implement rebalancing using calendar intervals, such as quarterly or semi-annually, or threshold triggers, such as when any individual position drifts more than 5% away from its target weight. Combining periodic reviews with rebalancing deposits ensures your portfolio remains calibrated to your intended risk tolerance without incurring excessive trading commissions.
