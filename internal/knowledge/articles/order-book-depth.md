## What is an Order Book?

An order book is a continuously updated electronic ledger displaying outstanding limit buy orders and sell orders for a specific financial instrument. The book is divided into two distinct columns: the bid side on the left, listing prices and quantities that buyers want to purchase, and the ask side on the right, displaying prices and quantities that sellers wish to offload. The gap between the best bid and best ask is the bid-ask spread.

## Understanding Market Depth

Market depth refers to the volume of orders resting at various price levels away from the best bid and ask. A "deep" order book features large share quantities queued at successive increments above and below the current market price. When a market possesses substantial depth, large institutional orders can execute without causing extreme price dislocations. Conversely, a "shallow" order book means that even moderate transactions will rapidly deplete available liquidity and trigger severe slippage.

## How Traders Read the Order Book

Traders analyze order book depth to gauge short-term supply and demand pressure. Massive clusters of buy orders at a specific round number can act as psychological price support, while thick blocks of sell orders can create resistance ceilings. However, modern automated algorithms frequently flash and cancel non-binding limit orders, so traders combine order book inspection with overall volume and historical support levels.
