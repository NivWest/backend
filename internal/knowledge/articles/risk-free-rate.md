## What is the Risk-Free Rate?

The risk-free rate of return is the theoretical rate of return an investor expects from an investment that bears zero credit or default risk over a given holding period. Because sovereign governments possess the authority to levy taxes and issue domestic currency, short-term government treasury bills—such as Swedish Riksbank bonds—are universally regarded as the practical proxy for risk-free assets.

## The Benchmark for All Asset Classes

The risk-free rate establishes the baseline hurdle rate that every other investment must exceed to justify taking on equity, credit, or liquidity risk. If a government bond pays a guaranteed 2.0% annual yield, an equity investment offering an expected return of only 3.0% provides an inadequate equity risk premium for enduring 20% annual volatility. In TradeEdu, performance analytics utilize the risk-free rate to calculate excess returns in the Sharpe ratio.

## Influence of Central Bank Monetary Policy

Central banks adjust benchmark interest rates to regulate inflation and economic growth. When central banks raise rates, the risk-free rate rises, making cash and money market yields increasingly attractive and exerting downward valuation pressure on riskier growth equities. Conversely, when rates drop near zero, investors are forced outward along the risk spectrum in search of meaningful returns.
