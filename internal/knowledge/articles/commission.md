## What is a Trading Commission?

A commission is a transaction fee charged by a brokerage to execute a buy or sell order on an exchange. Brokerages charge commissions to cover exchange membership fees, clearing infrastructure, regulatory reporting, and custodial operations. In TradeEdu, trading commissions mirror retail Nordic brokerage standards: each trade incurs a fee equal to the greater of 1.00 SEK or 0.25% of gross order value.

## The Impact of Minimum Fees on Small Trades

Because of minimum commission floors like 1.00 SEK, small trades carry a disproportionate percentage cost. If a beginner buys a single share of stock worth 10 SEK, paying a 1.00 SEK commission represents an immediate 10% drag on capital. Consequently, trading in larger lot sizes or grouping transactions together is essential to keep commission percentages aligned with the lower percentage tier rather than the minimum fixed fee.

## Accounting for Round-Trip Costs

Every round-trip trade involves two separate transactions: the initial entry and the eventual exit. Both sides incur commission charges. When evaluating potential trades, investors must calculate the total round-trip cost to determine the true breakeven price. Frequent trading can generate substantial cumulative fee friction, often turning modest gross gains into net losses after accounting for commissions and market spreads.
