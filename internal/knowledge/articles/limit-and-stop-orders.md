## Understanding Limit Orders

A limit order is an order to buy or sell a security at a specified price or better. A buy limit order executes only at the limit price or lower, protecting the buyer from paying unexpected market premiums. A sell limit order executes only at the limit price or higher. The primary tradeoff of a limit order is that execution is not guaranteed; if the market never touches your limit price, your order remains unfilled on the order book.

## Understanding Stop Orders

A stop order is an instruction to trigger a trade only after the market reaches a specified price level known as the stop price. Once the stock trades at or through the stop price, the stop order automatically becomes an active order. Stop-loss orders are widely employed by prudent traders to cap potential losses on existing positions by selling automatically when downward momentum breaches a critical threshold.

## Strategic Differences Between Limit and Stop Orders

While limit orders are designed to enter or exit at favorable prices during normal fluctuations, stop orders are designed for risk containment or breakout confirmation. Choosing between them depends on whether certainty of execution or certainty of price matters more for your trading thesis. In volatile market conditions, limit orders prevent severe slippage, whereas stop orders ensure quick exits when preservation of capital is paramount.
