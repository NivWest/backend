## What is a Market Order?

A market order is an instruction to buy or sell a security immediately at the best available current market price. Unlike limit orders that mandate an exact maximum or minimum execution price, a market order prioritizes speed and completeness of execution above price control. When an investor submits a market buy order, the brokerage routes it straight to the top of the order book to consume standing sell offers.

## When Market Orders Are Appropriate

Market orders are ideal when an investor needs guaranteed immediate execution and the underlying asset has exceptional liquidity and narrow bid-ask spreads. For prominent, heavily traded large-cap equities, market orders reliably fill within fractions of a percent from the quoted price. If a fast-moving news catalyst requires urgent entry or exit where missing the fill is far worse than a slight price discrepancy, market orders are the instrument of choice.

## The Inherent Danger in Illiquid Securities

In thinly traded stocks or during volatile market opens and earnings releases, market orders present extreme risks. Because market orders accept whatever price the prevailing order book offers, a large market buy order can chew through available asks and fill at severely inflated price levels. Traders must inspect order book depth and current spreads before choosing a market order over a limit order.
